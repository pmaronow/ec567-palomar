The compared result is `RankCertificates.infiniteGeometricClasses_exactRanksFiveThroughSeven` in [Solution.lean](Solution.lean#L18). It proves the conjunction of infinitude at each exact rank 5, 6 and 7, from one explicit argument `hGTZ`. The endpoint is literal elliptic-curve rank and geometric infinitude. In [Specification.lean](RankCertificates/Specification.lean#L29), `rationalRank` is `Module.rank ℤ E.toAffine.Point`, retaining its cardinal value. `HasExactRank` equates that cardinal with the specified natural number. `RationalEllipticCurve` includes nonsingularity, and `InfinitelyManyExactRank` requires infinitely many rational j-invariants of such models with the stated rank. `geometricallyIsomorphic_iff_j_eq` proves that these j-invariants classify isomorphism after base change to the algebraic closure of ℚ. Thus the target distinguishes infinitely many geometric classes of actual curves.

The external input, `ConditionalPrimePatterns.FiniteComplexityPrimeAsymptotic`, universally quantifies arbitrary twelve fixed integer affine forms and a fixed unit rectangle. Its hypotheses are nonzero gradients, pairwise nonzero determinants, simultaneous residue avoidance at every prime, uniform positive output growth, and the actual singular Euler product. It contains no elliptic family or rank predicate. Its conclusion is the actual twelvefold von Mangoldt sum minus σT² being o(T²). Pairwise nonparallelism supplies finite Cauchy–Schwarz complexity. This is a restricted corollary of Green–Tao's *Linear equations in primes* Main Theorem (2010), whose inverse-theorem and Möbius–nilsequence prerequisites were proved in 2012. The cited DOI records are 10.4007/annals.2010.171.1753, 10.4007/annals.2012.176.2.11, and 10.4007/annals.2012.175.2.3. The analytic theorem remains an explicit, unformalized input.

The three concrete specializations are `rank5_quadraticMass_of_finiteComplexityPrimeAsymptotic` and its rank-six and rank-seven counterparts. [PrimePatterns.lean](RankCertificates/PrimePatterns.lean) provides their literal output systems, determinant certificates and local admissibility. [SingularSeries.lean](RankCertificates/SingularSeries.lean#L549) defines the corrected residue densities and proves the three `rankN_singularSeries_hasProd_pos` certificates. At a prime p the factor is the simultaneous nonvanishing count divided by p² and by (1−1/p)¹²; every nonprime index contributes 1. [LiteralAffineSystems.lean](RankCertificates/LiteralAffineSystems.lean#L125) supplies rectangles [20T,21T]², [60T,61T]×[T,2T], and [8000T,8001T]×[T,2T], with every output at least T. Integer corners and affine interpolation justify positivity throughout the real rectangles; each real area is T². `FiniteComplexityPrimeAsymptotic.quadraticMass` converts the positive asymptotic into an eventual lower bound σT²/2.

The weighted mass yields genuine prime tuples through [PrimeExtraction.lean](RankCertificates/PrimeExtraction.lean#L324), especially `primePattern_infinite_of_scaled_quadratic_lower`. A positive Mangoldt factor initially allows prime powers. [ExtractionBounds.lean](RankCertificates/ExtractionBounds.lean) counts their nonprime support; [FiniteExclusions.lean](RankCertificates/FiniteExclusions.lean) bounds fixed exceptional values and coincident outputs. With outputs bounded by a fixed multiple of T, their weighted errors are respectively O(T³ᐟ² log¹³ T) and O(T log¹² T), hence o(T²), as proved in [ExtractionAsymptotics.lean](RankCertificates/ExtractionAsymptotics.lean). Enlarging the finite exclusion set defeats any purported finite collection of good tuples. The resulting `primePattern` requires twelve positive, pairwise distinct primes outside the prescribed small-prime sets. [LiteralPrimeBridge.lean](RankCertificates/LiteralPrimeBridge.lean#L40) converts these tuples to the exact `PrimeSpecialization5/6/7` hypotheses.
